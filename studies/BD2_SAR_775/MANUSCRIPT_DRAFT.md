# An Assay-Aware Structure–Activity Landscape of BRD4 Second Bromodomain Inhibitors

**Working manuscript, 22 November 2026.** Authors and final references to be completed after primary-source checks.

## Abstract

**Background:** Chemical changes in BRD4-BD2 inhibitors can produce different biochemical potency. Differences between assay methods can complicate comparisons. **Methods:** We investigated a curated set of 775 unique BRD4-BD2 structures with recorded biochemical IC50, assay and document identifiers. The analysis combined Bemis–Murcko scaffolds, ECFP4 radius-2 2048-bit fingerprints, similarity-defined candidate activity cliffs, RDKit single-cut matched molecular pairs and assay-stratified permutations. **Results:** The dataset contained 390 scaffolds (284 singletons), 79 assay identifiers, and 771 compounds with only one measurement. At Tanimoto >=0.80, 665 similar pairs included 78 candidate activity cliffs defined by >=10-fold IC50 difference; 63 shared an assay ID. Of 3,108 single-cut MMPs, 46 met the stringent shared-assay, >=0.80 similarity and >=10-fold difference criteria. Within-assay permutation testing (2,000 shuffles) gave mean 157.18 candidate cliffs versus 63 observed (lower-tail empirical p = 0.00050). Cliff percentages declined from 14.97% at Tanimoto >=0.70 to 6.45% at >=0.90. **Conclusion:** The landscape displays a tendency toward locally smooth structure–potency relationships alongside discrete candidate cliffs. The resulting candidate pairs warrant source-paper validation; single-measurement prevalence limits inference about experimental reproducibility.

## Introduction

BRD4 contains two bromodomains, BD1 and BD2, which have differing contributions to transcriptional regulation. Ligand-directed studies have discovered inhibitors of BD2, but numerical potency comparisons from heterogeneous assays require careful provenance control. Rather than fitting another QSAR model, this study tests whether molecular structural similarity is associated with biochemical potency similarity within assay groups and identifies matched chemical modifications with large potency changes.

## Methods

The source is the curated ChEMBL-derived 775-compound BRD4-BD2 strict modeling dataset. All 775 SMILES were parsed by RDKit. pIC50 and assay IDs were retained from the source. Murcko scaffolds and Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, 2048 bits) were generated. Candidate cliff criterion: ECFP4 Tanimoto >=0.80 and |delta pIC50|>=1. Matched molecular pairs were generated by single-cut RDKit fragmentation, selecting a common constant at least twice the size of the exchanged fragment and <=12 variable heavy atoms, and retaining one decomposition per molecule pair. Same-assay means >=1 common assay ID. A second test randomized potency labels within each single-assay group 2,000 times, preserving the assay-specific potency distribution, to construct a reference distribution of cliff counts. Similarity-threshold sensitivity was assessed from 0.70 to 0.90.

## Results

The 775 molecules represented 390 distinct scaffolds, 284 singletons and a largest scaffold family of 27 structures. The pIC50 range was 4.061–10.000 (median 6.785). There were 79 assay IDs; 771 compounds had one reported measurement. A total of 665 pairs met ECFP4 Tanimoto >=0.80, 78 qualified as candidate cliffs, and 63 of these shared an assay ID. The single-cut MMP screen yielded 3,108 pairs, of which 2,934 had an assay intersection. The stringent MMP-cliff intersection contained 46 pairs involving 66 distinct compounds from 12 assay IDs.

Among 635 close-analogue pairs in single-assay strata, 63 had >=10-fold potency differences; in 2,000 within-assay permutations, the reference mean was 157.18 (SD 11.22; 95% central interval 136–179), with lower-tail empirical p=0.00050. Cliff proportions fell with increasing similarity (0.70:14.97%; 0.75:12.98%; 0.80:9.92%; 0.85:7.26%; 0.90:6.45%).

## Discussion

These findings indicate a nonrandom association between molecular similarity and reported potency within ChEMBL assay groups, with a smaller subset of potential local SAR discontinuities. The test does **not** demonstrate that any individual transformation has a causal effect, that observed IC50 differences are independently replicated, or that an inhibitor has BD2-over-BD1 selectivity. The matched-pair and candidate-cliff tables provide prioritization sets for independent source review. The high proportion of single-record compounds and chemical-series clustering limit generalization.

- Obtain BD1 paired measurements before discussing domain selectivity.

## Verified primary-source cross-reference (22 November 2026)

BindingDB identifies assay CHEMBL5218148 as a BRD4-BD2 TR-FRET inhibition assay associated with Humphreys et al., *J Med Chem* (2022), on I-BET432. The source manuscript itself reports an activity cliff for N-linked imidazole 37 versus C-linked isomer 34 within its chemistry program; hence activity cliffs in related BET inhibitor medicinal chemistry are not an unreported phenomenon. Among the curated 63 shared-assay candidate cliffs, all pairs have matching document IDs, and 46 also satisfy the one-cut MMP criterion. The same-assay pair CHEMBL5219280 / CHEMBL5220623 differs by stereochemical assignment, with recorded IC50 63095.73 and 630.96 nM and equal achiral ECFP4 fingerprints; this is a *stereochemical candidate cliff* and should not be interpreted as an achiral single-cut chemical transformation.

## Verification of dominant source assays (22 November 2026)

Direct ChEMBL assay and document checks link 44 of the 63 candidate same-assay cliffs to four published medicinal-chemistry series: CHEMBL6086970 (17; Li et al., J Med Chem 2024, doi:10.1021/acs.jmedchem.4c02516, BRD4-BD2-selective discovery); CHEMBL5529441 (10; Li et al., J Med Chem 2024, doi:10.1021/acs.jmedchem.3c01837, BET-BD1-selective inhibitors with BD2 measured as a comparator); CHEMBL5376137 (nine; Bradley et al., J Med Chem 2023, doi:10.1021/acs.jmedchem.3c00906, BD1-selective chemical probe with BD2 comparator); CHEMBL4733660 (eight; Chen et al., Eur J Med Chem 2021, doi:10.1016/j.ejmech.2020.112868, dual HDAC/BRD4 inhibitors).

Assay procedures differ, including TR-FRET incubations of one hour versus 15 minutes and fluorescence-based formats. Four representative same-assay candidate-cliff pairs were checked against eight *original ChEMBL activity records*: CHEMBL6145355/CHEMBL6147019 (148.01/3.15 nM), CHEMBL5569832/CHEMBL5573825 (15.7/714.4 nM), CHEMBL5416784/CHEMBL5437203 (501.19/19952.62 nM), and CHEMBL4777392/CHEMBL4778531 (3.8/103.0 nM). All eight values match the curated table and use uncensored IC50 nM ('='). This validates traceability of four pairs, not experimental replication or novelty. Three of the four meet the operational single-cut MMP definition. Primary author-level compound-number comparisons and source-reported SAR remain to be checked across the other pairs.
